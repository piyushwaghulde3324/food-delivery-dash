1. Business problem

Repeat orders depend on ratings, and ratings depend on service quality. This report asks: when and where does delivery slip, how much does that cost in ratings, which cities and cuisines carry the demand, and do promotions earn their cost?

2. Dataset understanding

6,000 orders, 10 columns, one row per order, all order IDs unique.

Not available: discount amount, kitchen prep time, rider assignment time, cancellations, customer IDs.

3. Data cleaning and preparation

  • No missing values, no duplicate rows or order IDs.
  • City, cuisine, day and promo labels are consistent.
  • Outliers flagged by the IQR rule (19 order values, 25 delivery times, 33 distances, 13 ratings) were kept because they are plausible.
  • Implied rider speed averages 8 km/h and delivery time does not rise with distance, so the time likely includes kitchen and dispatch waiting.
  • Added fields: time slot (lunch 12 to 14 h, dinner 19 to 21 h, other), late flag (over 45 min), low-rating flag (under 3.5), delivery and distance bands.

4. Key insights

  • Dinner is the breaking point. 39.7 min against 33.7 elsewhere. 21.2% of dinner orders are late, against 5.5% otherwise.
  • Slow delivery lowers ratings. Correlation -0.35, about 0.023 rating points per extra minute. Under 25 min averages 4.38; over 50 min averages 3.68.
  • Mumbai is the weak link. 27% of orders and revenue, slowest (38.7 min), lowest rating (4.04), 35% of its dinner orders late. Delhi follows at 11.9% late.
  • Demand is concentrated. Lunch and dinner windows hold 80% of orders in 6 of 15 hours; Friday to Sunday hold 55%.
  • Hour 22 proves capacity can hold. 387 orders, still 33.2 min on average.
  • Promos show no lift. ₹378 vs ₹381 basket, 4.08 vs 4.10 rating, neither difference significant. Promo share is about 30% in every hour and city.
  • Distance is not the driver. Delivery time is flat across distance bands. Biryani at dinner is slowest at 42.3 min.

6. Business impact

  • 615 orders (10.3%) took over 45 min, carrying ₹2.35 lakh, about 10% of order value, at a rating near 3.8.
  • Bringing dinner delivery to the off-peak speed would cut late dinner orders from 21% to about 6%, roughly 285 fewer late orders in this sample.
  • By the rating-vs-time slope, that is about +0.14 on dinner ratings and +0.04 overall. This is an estimate from correlation, not proof of cause.
  • About a third of orders use a promo with no visible gain. Discount cost is not in the data, so savings cannot be computed.

7. Recommendations and conclusion

  1. Add rider supply at 19 to 21 h, Mumbai and Delhi first. Target dinner late orders under 10%.
  2. Audit Mumbai dispatch rules and restaurant prep times.
  3. Stop blanket promos at dinner; use them in quiet hours and Mon to Wed, with holdout tests.
  4. Review Biryani prep and packing at dinner.
  5. Alert customers and reroute at 40 min, before orders turn late.
  6. Capture discount value, prep time, rider assignment time, cancellations and customer IDs.

Conclusion: order value, cuisine, day and distance barely change satisfaction. Delivery speed does, and it fails in one window: dinner, most of all in Mumbai. Fix evening capacity first and make promos earn their place.
